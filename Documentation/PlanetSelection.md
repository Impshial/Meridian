# Procedural planet selection

The planet targets naturalistic orbital geography and balanced habitable planets with approximately 25–45% continental land coverage. The background is black and the camera is fixed. Milestone 3 enables CONTINUE after land selection and adds the [landing survey](LandingSiteSelection.md).

## Controls and transitions

NEW COLONY immediately displays `Loading Exo-planet...`, blocks repeated activation and fades the complete menu to black over 0.5 seconds. The loading text is independent of cover alpha and survives scene changes. A fresh setup session generates a new seed on a cancellable worker, then creates Unity resources on the main thread. Destination readiness, fade completion and initiating-input release gate interaction. Opening PlanetSelection directly creates a session if needed.

Left-drag rotates the planet around camera-relative axes; no camera transform changes, idle rotation, pan, inertia, or keyboard viewing controls are used. A 7-pixel threshold at 1080p scales with viewport height and tracks maximum excursion. UI owns an entire gesture begun on a button. Focus loss clears captured input. Scroll uses Input System 1.20's normalized ticks, with a 120-unit divisor only if native Windows scroll mode is explicitly selected.

Zoom changes perspective field of view. Projected full globe diameter starts at 63% of viewport height, with a 45–210% range. Close views intentionally crop the globe, including at narrow aspect ratios; neither the flag nor bottom controls constrain magnification. Each normalized wheel tick multiplies target framing by exp(0.10), approximately 10.5%, followed by unscaled exponential smoothing. Extreme input clamps safely. Wheel input over either button is ignored. The camera stays at (0, 0, -4), looking toward the origin; the globe stays at unit scale. The two overlay buttons have local translucent black backgrounds for contrast over terrain. A flag can leave the viewport without moving its geographic anchor.

A legitimate click captures the visible globe collider hit in local coordinates, shows a short screen-space ring and rotates that point to screen centre over 0.65 seconds. Dragging cancels centering; camera pose and zoom are unchanged. Water clicks show neutral feedback and center the water without replacing selected land; background/UI/drag releases do neither. Land clicks plant one flag, with an invariant surface anchor separate from its planting/facing pivots. The visual leans 30 degrees and stays approximately 34–56 pixels tall. A noninteractive edge indicator represents a hidden/offscreen selection, with a `Far side` cue when appropriate; the real anchor never moves to the front.

BACK ends the setup and returns to MainMenu. CONTINUE is enabled only after land selection; it captures the current rotation/zoom and displays `Loading Landing Site...` before opening the survey. BACK TO PLANET restores the retained maps/data and selected flag. Failures show a readable explanation and return action. Neither screen accepts input during loading/fades.

## Geographic representation and budget

- Generator version: `meridian-planet-2`. Version 2 updates surface interpolation, appearance baking and boundary sampling. The seeded graph's elevation, climate, drainage, basins and river routes remain byte-identical to version 1. Reproducibility requires the same seed, captured `PlanetParameters`, and generator version; no dependency on Unity's global random state or frame timing.
- Geography: closed subdivided icosphere graph, level 6, 40,962 samples / 81,920 graph faces. Its graph is CPU data, not rendered terrain.
- Rendering and collision: level-4 icosphere, 2,562 vertices / 5,120 triangles. Relief is capped at 0.5% of the unit radius by default. Forests are regional surface detail, not tree objects.
- Maps: three 4096 × 2048 RGBA32 textures by default. Land color/bathymetry is sRGB, normals/vegetation and boundary/classification/temperature are linear. Color and normals have 13 mip levels, trilinear filtering and anisotropy 4; the categorical surface map has one level and bilinear filtering for its continuous channels. All wrap U and clamp V. GPU pixel payload is approximately 117.33 MiB; Unity's runtime texture profiler reports approximately 234.71 MiB including its native allocation accounting on the tested DX12 backend.
- Appearance sampling uses screen-derived gradients computed from continuous local direction, avoiding the longitude wrap discontinuity. Filtered normals are decoded and normalized. Surface R is a continuous signed boundary field sampled at LOD 0, G is a discrete water-type ID, B a discrete biome ID and A temperature remapped from the freezing interval 0.08–0.16 onto 0–1. This gives ice transitions all 256 levels instead of approximately 20; full climate temperatures remain in the graph. G describes the nearest water type on a dry-side edge as well, allowing correct inland-water antialiasing. IDs use explicit nearest-texel loads. Picking matches R's texel-center bilinear sampling and 0.5 threshold. Final visual coverage blends over approximately one output pixel; it does not widen or blur the authoritative boundary.
- The worker's three map arrays occupy 96 MiB while baking. After upload, the two appearance arrays are released; only the 32 MiB surface array and existing graph fields remain. There are no full-resolution elevation/moisture arrays or normal-height scratch map. `Sample()` obtains physical elevation/moisture directly from the original graph, independent of appearance resolution.
- `PlanetData` owns the seed, parameters, elevation, moisture, temperature, terrain biomes, filled drainage heights, downstream links, accumulation, lakes/rivers, and raster fields. `PlanetData.Sample(localUnitDirection)` is the common geographic interface. `PlanetSelection` retains the seed/version, normalized local direction, and sampled geographic character in memory only.

Continents combine domain-warped 3D noise and a seed-dependent sea-level quantile. Interior ridge belts add mountains above broad low-relief land. Moisture, temperature, latitude, and elevation determine biome blends. A stable priority flood resolves drainage over depressions; reverse flow accumulation identifies connected river paths. Large bounded depressions become lakes. Deterministic basin/climate corrections supply missing feature examples, followed by drainage recalculation where needed. This is an orbital approximation, not an erosion or climate simulator.

River half-width is independent of texture dimensions, preserving the original minimum of `2π / 1024 × 0.9` radians (approximately 0.00552). Higher resolution therefore refines the edge without shrinking rivers. Segment candidates include adjacent graph faces, preserving channel continuity. Width remains a symbolic orbital convention, not a physical river-width measurement. The black generation interval lasts until completion; there is no additional fixed wait or loading screen UI. See the measured generation/frame costs in [validation](Validation.md).

## Regional terrain appearance

Each map texel evaluates the procedural fields at its own spherical direction; the source is not an enlarged old bitmap. Bounded cubic interpolation with shared edge controls rounds graph-scale coastal, lake and freezing contours without overshooting node extrema. Broad graph heights and climate values remain unchanged. Shared vertex gradients smooth terrain normals across graph faces. Analytic 3D noise gradients supply shading detail without a resolution-dependent finite-difference gain, water-edge height discontinuity, or polar tangent singularity.

Fresh 3D noise in the bake supplies regional color/normal variation. Additional seed-offset 3D surface detail in the shader gives vegetation and dry ground finer shading at regional zoom; its amplitude fades with the screen footprint to prevent unresolved detail from crawling. Neither layer changes geometry or future placement elevations. Forest/rock/snow blends remain regional, with smoothly varying vegetation strength in normal-map alpha.

4096 × 2048 plus procedural detail was selected after native 1920 × 1080 and 2560 × 1440 inspection at 210% zoom. Map width remains configurable from 1024 to 8192, snapped to powers of two. 8192 is supported by the settings clamp but was not needed or benchmarked for this delivery; it quadruples map pixel payload. Geometry remains independently configurable and unchanged at 5,120 triangles.

Baking uses deterministic parallel rows with at most eight workers, checks cancellation within rows, and uploads Unity textures on the main thread. Textures are created uninitialized, fully populated and mipmapped before the globe is revealed. Rendering, dragging and zooming never rebake or upload static maps. [Unity mipmap guidance](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.5/Documentation/Manual/texture-mipmaps-introduction.html) and [Texture2D construction](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.5/Documentation/ScriptReference/Texture2D-ctor.html) describe the filtering, color-space and allocation options used here.

## Water presentation

Liquid water uses three octaves of continuous object-space 3D noise gradients, projected into the sphere's tangent plane. Slow phase movement animates the normals without moving the mesh or geographic data. Screen derivatives attenuate unresolved octaves; there is no longitude seam, polar tangent singularity, or camera-attached texture. Spatially varied roughness breaks up a restrained GGX sun glint, with dielectric Fresnel response and a subtle grazing reflection tint. Existing ocean bathymetry in color-map alpha controls shallow/deep color. Lakes and rivers receive 30% ripple amplitude and 45% glint strength; the existing temperature-based ice treatment is retained.

`Art/Materials/PlanetSurface.mat` retains six water controls: ripple strength (0.13), detail scale (115), animation speed (0.22), roughness min/max (0.23/0.40), glint strength (0.85), and depth-color contribution (0.9). Water adds no textures, meshes, render targets or per-frame uploads. The new surface coverage and discrete type lookup feed the existing water treatment; color-map alpha still carries ocean bathymetry.

The 210% setting corresponds to a 14.093° vertical field of view at the fixed camera distance, versus 44.786° initially. It provides about 3.23 times the old effective 65% closest framing. This remains an orbital surface: added terrain and water detail represents regional appearance rather than physical trees, rocks or waves. Local terrain is still future work.

## Coordinates and future terrain

North is +Y. Longitude zero points along +Z, and positive longitude turns toward +X. For a normalized local direction, longitude = atan2(x, z), latitude = asin(y), U = longitude / (2π) + 0.5, and V = latitude / π + 0.5. U wraps. Exact north/south directions are shared by all longitudes at the poles. The spherical graph has ordinary neighbours across the longitude seam and near both poles; rasterization uses the same directions as the shader and picker.

Planet selection identifies a region. Milestone 3's surface generator consumes its water boundaries, drainage routes, climate and graph elevations, then measures buildable area and full-footprint placement on a four-tile survey. The globe's visual relief remains separate from physical terrain height. See the surface document for mapping scale, readiness and expansion boundaries; there is no construction or survival simulation yet.

## Ownership and authoring

The scene owns its task, interaction state and flag. SetupSession owns the authoritative planet data and one PlanetRenderResources cache (mesh, material and three textures) across surface visits. Globe scenes detach collider/renderer references when unloaded; returning reattaches that cache without needing released appearance arrays or duplicating maps. Ending the setup releases the cache. Surface rendering resources are scene-owned, while logical region data and the candidate/confirmation remain in the session. The temporary transition overlay survives only loading/fades or a recoverable error. The player has no editor service or network listener.

The original foundation authoring command is guarded against overwriting milestone 2. The planet authoring command incrementally wires the existing NEW COLONY button and creates this milestone's saved assets. Neither authoring command runs automatically when opening or playing the project.
